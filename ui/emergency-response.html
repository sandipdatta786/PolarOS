Why This Matters
At -40°C with a medical emergency, every minute counts. A person in cardiac arrest or severe hypothermia has a narrow survival window. This system answers the critical question in seconds—is there a safe helicopter window?—instead of after manual checking.

Without this system, the answer comes from someone at the radio doing mental math about airstrip distance, weather, and ship location. With this system, it's automated, always current, and always correct.

Emergency Procedures (Readable Offline)

Tap any card to see the step-by-step procedure. All procedures are stored locally and work with zero internet.

🏥
Medical Emergency
Person unresponsive, critical injury, or severe illness
  1. Call medical officer immediately (radio channel 3)
  2. Check airway, breathing, circulation (ABC)
  3. Move to heated shelter if safe to do so
  4. Begin CPR if no pulse (start compressions)
  5. Tap red MEDEVAC button on comms—check evacuation window
  6. Prepare landing area for helicopter
  7. Document time, vital signs, actions taken
🔥
Fire
Uncontrolled fire inside or near structure
  1. Sound alarm (three long blasts on whistle)
  2. Evacuate building immediately
  3. Muster at designated assembly point (north of station)
  4. Report to fire warden—confirm all personnel accounted for
  5. Small fire (<1 m²): use CO₂ extinguisher, keep 2m distance
  6. Large fire: DO NOT attempt to extinguish—wait for evacuation
  7. Fire chief activates MEDEVAC if injuries present
🚐
Vehicle Stuck/Broken
Vehicle immobilized in field or failing critical systems
  1. Stop immediately—do not attempt risky recovery
  2. Report location and status to base (radio)
  3. Stay inside vehicle (better shelter than outside)
  4. Activate emergency beacon (EPIRB) if available
  5. Ration fuel for heating—vent exhaust to prevent CO poisoning
  6. Base will dispatch rescue. ETA 2–4 hours typically
  7. Monitor radio every 15 minutes for updates
⛽
Fuel Leak / Spillage
Diesel or aviation fuel leaking from tank or container
  1. Evacuate area immediately (fuel is flammable)
  2. No smoking, no ignition sources within 50m
  3. Contain spill: use absorbent matting or sand
  4. Contact logistics officer—assess if it impacts fuel rations
  5. If >100L spilled: prepare inventory alert (notify MEDEVAC system)
  6. Document spill: volume, location, containment time
  7. Large spill = potential emergency if it impacts diesel budget
❄️
Crevasse / Fall
Person falls into crevasse or ice field
  1. DO NOT attempt solo rescue—call base immediately
  2. Throw rope to person (they must tie in)
  3. Keep calm. Crevasse rescue takes 30–60 minutes
  4. Base will dispatch rescue team with pulley system
  5. Team will raise person using Z-pulley mechanical advantage
  6. Evacuate person to medical for hypothermia/injury check
  7. Document: depth fallen, duration in crevasse, injuries
📡
Communication Failure
Radio/satellite comms are down—cannot reach base
  1. Try backup radio (second transceiver in each team kit)
  2. Climb highest point in area—signal improves with altitude
  3. Try again in 30 minutes (satellite passes rotate)
  4. Activate EPIRB if available (does not need working radio)
  5. If field party: retrace route back to station
  6. Mark position clearly—mark with flag or marker
  7. Base will initiate search if team overdue AND no check-in
⚡
Why Offline Procedures Matter
In an emergency, there's no time to wait for a satellite link or scroll through a cloud dashboard. Every procedure on this page is stored locally—downloaded at season start, updated at every sync.

A team out on the ice doesn't wait for the internet. They open this app, tap the procedure, and follow the steps. Offline-first design isn't a feature for nice days. It's survival.

All procedures are based on Antarctic expedition best practices.
